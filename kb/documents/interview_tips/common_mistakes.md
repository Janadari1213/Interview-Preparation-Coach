## Common Candidate Mistake 1: Failing to Research the Company
Candidates often show up with vague knowledge of what the company does or what product features they offer. Demonstrating a clear understanding of the company's mission, market position, and recent news shows genuine interest and sets top applicants apart.

## Common Candidate Mistake 2: Giving Generic or Unsubstantiated Answers
Answering questions with high-level buzzwords like "I am a hard worker and team player" without providing concrete examples weakens your credibility. Always back up your statements with specific achievements, metrics, or personal experiences.

## Common Candidate Mistake 3: Bad-Mouthing Past Employers or Teammates
Speaking negatively about previous supervisors, colleagues, or company cultures reflects poorly on your professional emotional intelligence. Frame past challenges constructively by focusing on problem-solving, resilience, and personal growth.

## Common Candidate Mistake 4: Not Asking Thoughtful Questions at the End
When asked "Do you have any questions for us?", responding with "No, I think you covered everything" signals a lack of curiosity or preparation. Prepare 3-5 insightful questions about team dynamics, technical challenges, or growth opportunities.

## Common Candidate Mistake 5: Ramble Without Structuring Answers
Failing to organize your response often leads to long-winded answers that lose the interviewer's attention. Use frameworks like STAR for behavioral questions or top-down summaries for technical design questions to stay concise.

## Common Candidate Mistake 6: Ignoring Clarifying Questions in Technical Rounds
Jumping straight into writing code or architecture diagrams without asking clarifying questions is a major red flag in technical interviews. Interviewers evaluate your thought process and problem definition skills as much as your final solution.
